A food-waste & packaging project

The part you throw away
is the part we need.

Every watermelon leaves behind a thick white rind and a pile of seeds, usually landfill by dinner. We turn the rind into candy, and turn gelatin into edible film that replaces single-use plastic. Nothing wasted, twice over.

The products

two of our cool recipez :O

Product 01 — Edible

Watermelon Rind Candy

The white layer between the green skin and red juicyyy flesh, usually scraped into the trash, but we candied them into a chewy and sweet candyy >:D

  1. Remove the green outer skin, cut the white rind into small pieces.
  2. Boil the rind pieces until soft.
  3. Cook in sugar syrup until fully absorbed.
  4. Dry the candy before packaging.
Product 02 — Packaging

Edible / Biodegradable Plastic

Gelatin, glycerin, and water form a flexible edible film that can wrap the candy itself, a surprisingly sweet packaging material that breaks down instead of piling up :O

  1. Mix water, gelatin, and glycerin.
  2. Heat until the gelatin fully dissolves.
  3. Pour into a flat mold.
  4. Dry 24–48 hours until a flexible film forms.
The science

What's actually happening, by subject

The rind is genuinely edible

The white rind carries fiber, vitamin C, and pectin, it's not waste by nature, just waste by habit (ofc because it doesnt taste good), those same properties are what let it hold structure through boiling and candying ><

Sugar as a preservative

A high sugar concentration limits the water available for microbes to grow in, which is why the candy keeps without refrigeration once fully dried ^^

Osmosis makes the candy

Sugar moves into the rind while water moves out (yeah it did switch), following the concentration gradient. The result is a piece of rind that's sweeter, denser, and more shelf-stable than where it started :O

Gelatin as a polymer

Gelatin proteins link into a tangled network as they cool, forming a film. Glycerin sits between those strands as a plasticizer, keeping the film bendable instead of brittle.

Heat transfer

Even heat is what cooks the rind through and dissolves gelatin completely, uneven heat leaves lumps in the film or unevenly candied rind.

Evaporation shapes the film

As the poured mixture dries, water leaves the surface first. How fast and how evenly it evaporates determines whether the final film is strong and flexible or thin and prone to tearing ^o^
